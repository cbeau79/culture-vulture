# Culture Digest — 10 July 2026

A folk-heavy week: two quietly devastating singer-songwriter records, a Squid guitarist gone ambient, plus Iñárritu's *Amores Perros* landing on MUBI today at 25, a labyrinthine new Ridgway, and a Surrealist painter worth rediscovering.

## 🎵 Music
- **Anton Pearson — *Driving Through Belgium*** — The Squid guitarist's ambient side project reaches back through a deep tradition of British music shaped by the countryside. The Quietus's album of the week, and squarely in your Frahm/Hopkins pocket.
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus — "Nature Boy"](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/album-of-the-week/driving-through-belgium-anton-pearson-review/)
- **Tony Njoku — *A World of Bodies on Fire*** — The Black British experimentalist abandons his old maximalism for a complete reset: meditations for synth and piano in which he rediscovers his voice and lets go of frustration.
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus — "Quiet Chaos"](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/album-of-the-week/a-world-of-bodies-on-fire-by-tony-njoku-review/)
- **Leah Senior — *Pt. Roadknight*** — The Melbourne songwriter leans into the hazy fog of late-'60s/early-'70s folk rock, recorded with a warm analogue texture; an inward gaze turned outward "with rare grace." (She keeps it off Spotify, fittingly.)
  Read/Watch: [KLOF Mag review](https://klofmag.com/2026/07/leah-senior-pt-roadknight/)
- **Tucker Zimmerman — *Dream Me a Dream*** — The final record from the folk outsider, who died with his wife in a house fire in January. Moody folk-pop dashed with synths, an Ondes Martenot and Jackie Oates' fiddle — "just as strong a hello as a goodbye."
  Read/Watch: [Uncut review (8/10)](https://www.uncut.co.uk/reviews/tucker-zimmermans-dream-me-a-dream-reviewed-life-affirming-farewell-from-the-folk-outsider-155126/)

## 🎨 Art
- **Gertrude Abercrombie and her Midwest magic realists** — A pair of Milwaukee shows resurface the "queen of the bohemian artists" and the circle of Surrealist friends around her — spare, spooky, self-mythologising paintings full of owls, moons and empty rooms.
  Read/Watch: [Hyperallergic review](https://hyperallergic.com/gertrude-abercrombies-band-of-midwest-magic-realists/) (paywall)
- **Lyndon Barrois's "sportraits"** — The animator sculpts World Cup history — political protest, the fight for equal rights, iconic moments FIFA would rather forget — entirely out of chewing-gum wrappers. Odd, painstaking and genuinely moving to look at.
  Read/Watch: [Hyperallergic feature](https://hyperallergic.com/the-artist-sculpting-world-cup-history-out-of-gum-wrappers/)
- **The pessimism of André Breton** — A reconsideration of *Nadja* and the Surrealist project: magic, chance and disenchantment in the founder who could never quite believe in his own optimism.
  Read/Watch: [Los Angeles Review of Books essay](https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/andre-breton-nadja-magic-art-cavalier-perspective-pessimism/)

## 📚 Books & Writing
- **Keith Ridgway — *Dooneen*** — The Irish writer's most beguiling novel yet: a man slips through the bushes of a south London park and out into a dreamlike Dublin, in a labyrinth of unstable places and Alice-in-Wonderland sleights. Longing and human connection underneath it all.
  Read/Watch: [Irish Times interview](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/books/2026/06/13/author-keith-ridgway-dooneen-is-the-most-beautiful-place-on-earth-as-far-as-im-concerned/)
- **Roshan Sethi — *The Simp: A Novel Without a Hero*** — A deadpan, stinging Hollywood satire in which race and colonial history collide with the dream of fame — "exceedingly smart and funny," per Sam Lipsyte.
  Read/Watch: [New York Times Book Review](https://www.nytimes.com/2026/07/06/books/review/roshan-sethi-the-simp.html) (paywall)
- **Rachel Aviv — *You Won't Get Free of It*** — The *Strangers to Ourselves* author on mothers and daughters, moving between reportage and interior life like third-person fiction. "No one who reads Aviv's book will ever read Alice Munro the same way again."
  Read/Watch: [4Columns review](https://www.4columns.org/beller-thomas/you-wont-get-free-of-it)
- **Tana French — *The Keeper*** — The grand finale of the Cal Hooper trilogy: a retired Chicago cop turned rural-Irish transplant, a dead young woman, village feuds and local power. A slow burn built from small details and superbly shaded dialogue — noir on the crime shelf you've been circling.
  Read/Watch: [NPR review](https://www.npr.org/2026/03/31/nx-s1-5763821/the-keeper-tana-french-review)

## 🎬 Film & TV
- **Amores Perros — 25th-anniversary 4K restoration** — Iñárritu's ferocious debut, three lives bound by one Mexico City car crash, lands on MUBI today in a Criterion-supervised 4K. If you've never seen it, this is the week.
  Read/Watch: [NPR — "The second life of a classic"](https://www.npr.org/2026/06/17/nx-s1-5793824/amores-perros-remastered-theaters-re-release)
- **The Drama** (dir. Kristoffer Borgli) — Now on streaming: Zendaya and Robert Pattinson as an engaged couple derailed by one revelation. An audaciously uncomfortable morality play with career-highlight performances.
  Read/Watch: [RogerEbert.com review](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-drama-robert-pattinson-zendaya-a24-film-review-2026)
- **Phantoms of July** (dir. Julian Radlmaier) — A wry, culturally rich midsummer-day's-dream of a film: an East German waitress and an Iranian YouTuber, both stuck, drawn into a ghost hunt. Deadpan comedy and real political weight in the same breath.
  Read/Watch: [The Film Verdict review](https://thefilmverdict.com/phantoms-of-july/)
- **The House of the Spirits** (Prime Video) — The first Spanish-language adaptation of Isabel Allende's novel: three generations of Trueba women, sumptuous, brutal and genuinely magical. Overdue and, per Ebert, done right.
  Read/Watch: [RogerEbert.com review](https://www.rogerebert.com/streaming/house-of-the-spirits-isabel-allende-prime-video-tv-review-2026)
