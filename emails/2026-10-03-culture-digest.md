# Culture Digest — 3 October 2026

A week of hymns and reckonings: Susanne Sundfør sings a medieval mystic over a church organ, Patrick deWitt sends a draft-dodger north in a Jaguar, Alan Hollinghurst reads Peter Hujar's photographs, and Salman Rushdie returns to the stage in Alex Gibney's *Knife*.

## 🎵 Music

- **Susanne Sundfør & Kit Downes — *Revelations of Divine Love*** — Out 2 Oct on Bella Union: recorded live in a Norwegian church with only her voice and a pipe organ, setting the words of the 14th-century mystic Julian of Norwich. Austere and spectral; start with "What Are We".
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus — Jeremy Allen on *Revelations of Divine Love*](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/susanne-sundfor-revelations-of-divine-love/)
- **Emily A. Sprague — *Cyano*** — Out 2 Oct on RVNG Intl: the Florist songwriter sings over her modular-synth ambient work for the first time, on a 14-track concept album about an imagined planet "destroyed by misunderstanding", its sounds weathered like a transmission left in a puddle. Try "Spiraling to the Place".
  Read/Watch: [Post-Trash — feature interview on *Cyano* (no in-depth review yet)](http://post-trash.com/news/2026/9/29/spiraling-to-the-place-emily-a-sprague-imagines-an-entire-planet-on-cyano-feature-interview)
- **Vanishing Twin — *Archives*** — Out 2 Oct on Fire: the London trio built it remotely, between rural France and a boat on a London waterway, out of homemade instruments, chopped samples and slow dub. The Quietus hears "Chiastic Slide-era Autechre reimagining The Wicker Man OST"; try "Hazama".
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus, Album of the Week — Will Ainsley on *Archives*](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/album-of-the-week/vanishing-twin-archives-review/)
- **Greg Freeman — *All Set the Bone*** — Out 2 Oct on Transgressive/Canvasback: the Vermont songwriter's John Congleton-produced follow-up to the Best New Music-rated *Burnover*. His rowdiest record of ragged americana, centred on the anti-chorus rant of "Culture".
  Read/Watch: [Stereogum, Album of the Week — Danielle Chelosky on *All Set the Bone*](https://stereogum.com/2512728/album-of-the-week-greg-freeman-all-set-the-bone/reviews/album-of-the-week)
- **Jeremy Cunningham — *Passing Fires*** — Out 2 Oct on Northern Spy: the Chicago drummer's third album, with Jeff Parker and Makaya McCraven among the cast. It runs from jazz/post-rock to the tender title track, which has recordings from his family's living room, and it is built from grief and memory.
  Read/Watch: [Echoes and Dust — Ljubinko Zivkovic on *Passing Fires*](https://echoesanddust.com/2026/09/jeremy-cunningham-passing-fires/)

## 🎨 Art

- **Peter Hujar and Paul Thek, by Alan Hollinghurst** — The novelist reviews Andrew Durbin's dual biography of the photographer and the sculptor and reads Hujar's pictures closely. In the 1970 Gay Liberation Front photograph he notes that the lesbians make up the front row. A reflective essay on the years just before AIDS.
  Read/Watch: [NYRB — Alan Hollinghurst, "The Elusive Glamour of Peter and Paul"](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2026/10/22/the-elusive-glamour-of-peter-hujar-and-paul-thek/)
- **Ithell Colquhoun, by Marina Warner** — A long essay on the British Surrealist and occultist. Warner argues Colquhoun's originality lay in carrying chance techniques like her "parsemage" (floating pigment on water and lifting it off with paper) into her fiction, and includes a wonderfully polite 1948 BBC demonstration of it.
  Read/Watch: [NYRB — Marina Warner, "Possessed of Alchemystic Powers"](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2026/10/22/possessed-of-alchemystic-powers-ithell-colquhoun/)
- **Arthur Jafa — *I Am Tony*** — The novelist Stephanie Wambugu gives Jafa's biggest survey a mixed verdict. She praises his gallows humour and instinct for virality, starting from a wallpaper of 19th-century Black schoolchildren whose flag salute looks like a Sieg Heil, but asks whether his obliqueness sells the work short.
  Read/Watch: [Hyperallergic — Stephanie Wambugu, "Arthur Jafa, King of Pop"](https://hyperallergic.com/arthur-jafa-king-of-pop/) (paywall — free sign-in)

## 📚 Books & Writing

- **Patrick deWitt — *Dodge City*** — 4th Estate, 29 Sep: in 1967 a UCLA dropout with a draft notice and a bag of amphetamines drives an E-Type to the Canadian border, settling family scores on the way. As funny and stylised as *The Sisters Brothers*, but his tenderest book yet.
  Read/Watch: [The Guardian — Keshava Guha's review](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2026/oct/01/dodge-city-by-patrick-dewitt-review-a-wild-draft-dodging-road-trip)
- **T. Geronimo Johnson — *The Occidental Book of the Dead*** — Little, Brown, out now (US 6 Oct; 672pp): a Black Atlanta cop covers for his white rookie's shooting of a teenager, and halfway through the novel turns into a first-person confession. CrimeReads calls it the literary crime epic of the year — Ellroy-grade police corruption.
  Read/Watch: [Boston Globe — Michael Schaub, "a tour de force"](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/01/arts/t-geronimo-johnson-occidental-book-of-the-dead/)
- **Hernan Diaz — *Ply*** — Picador, 24 Sep: the *Trust* author turns to literary sci-fi. In a collapsed future New York, an orphan who steals power from the grid is drawn into an experiment to alter reality. Reviews are sharply split, and this one is honest about the lecture-heavy second half.
  Read/Watch: [Boston Globe — Priscilla Gilman's review](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/09/22/arts/hernan-diaz-ply-novel/)
- **Andrea Wulf — *The Traveller*** — Shortlisted for the Baillie Gifford Prize on 1 Oct: the *Invention of Nature* author on George Forster, who sailed with Cook at 17, argued against Kant's racial theories, and died a revolutionary in Paris in 1794. Big-ideas Enlightenment history.
  Read/Watch: [ICWA Compass — Anna Gielas, "A man of no nation"](https://compass.icwa.org/p/review-a-man-of-no-nation)

## 🎬 Film & TV

- **Knife: The Attempted Murder of Salman Rushdie (Alex Gibney — UK & Irish cinemas from 2 Oct; US widening)** — Rushdie reads his memoir over hospital footage shot by his wife, the poet Rachel Eliza Griffiths. The ending cuts real footage of the attack against his return to the stage a year later.
  Read/Watch: [Sight and Sound — Philip Concannon's review](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/knife-attempted-murder-salman-rushdie-fascinating-documentary-captures-authors-courage-humour-wake-brutal-attack)
- **Teenage Sex and Death at Camp Miasma (Jane Schoenbrun — streaming on MUBI from 2 Oct)** — The *I Saw the TV Glow* director's Queer Palm winner: Hannah Einbinder plays a director remaking a cult slasher who tracks down its original star (Gillian Anderson). A meta film about watching and desire, "wise, kind, and playful about sex and cinema".
  Read/Watch: [Sight and Sound — Blake Simons's review](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/teenage-sex-death-camp-miasma-jane-schoenbruns-open-hearted-slasher-playful-about-sex-cinema)
- **War (George Kay — Sky Atlantic/NOW from 2 Oct; HBO from 1 Oct)** — A London divorce between a tech billionaire (Dominic West) and a fading star (Sienna Miller), fought out mostly through the two law firms. Polished and sharply written, with a strong bench of supporting actors; the week's notable UK drama.
  Read/Watch: [TheWrap — Diedre Johnson's review](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/tv-shows/war-hbo-sky-review-sienna-miller-dominic-west/)
- **Fjord (Cristian Mungiu — US cinemas from 9 Oct; UK 13 Nov after LFF)** — This year's Palme d'Or: Sebastian Stan and Renate Reinsve play a devout couple whose children are taken by Norwegian child services. The year's big argument film; this review is a sceptical one, which is the point.
  Read/Watch: [Sight and Sound — Sophie Monks Kaufman's review from Cannes](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/fjord-curiously-underdeveloped-addition-cristian-mungius-body-work-explores-religious-persecution-small-scandinavian-town)
