## 🎨 Art
- **The Starry Night, dated by the stars** — A Kyung Hee University team used shape-matching to argue that the star cluster in Van Gogh's painting is the Hyades, with a waning crescent moon and Venus, as seen before dawn over Saint-Rémy around 22 July 1889, a month later than the standard June dating, suggesting he conceived the picture in June and finished it later. The lead author concedes "further research in art history and conservation science could shed more light", which is the right note of caution for a lovely piece of detective work.
  Read/Watch: [Chelsea Gohd's report on the paper, Space.com](https://www.space.com/astronomy/van-goghs-the-starry-night-depicts-the-sky-on-july-22-1889-researchers-find)
- **L.S. Lowry, impersonal effects** — Matthew Sperling in Apollo makes an argument about the pictures rather than the show: Lowry packs his scenes with people yet has almost no interest in them as individuals, treating figures as interchangeable units while his viewpoint rises over the decades from street level to industrial vistas in which people shrink to specks. "Lowry can identify the masses without necessarily identifying with them." The closing pages on the "Ann" portraits reveal "a much weirder and darker artist than he's generally thought to be".
  Read/Watch: [Matthew Sperling, "Impersonal effects: the crowded visions of L.S. Lowry", Apollo](https://apollo-magazine.com/lowry-theatre-of-life-mk-gallery-exhibition-review/)
- **The many views of Yvonne Jacquette** — Max Norman's essay on the late painter's aerial pictures, made from aeroplane windows, helicopters and high-rise rooms: loose cloud and highway studies giving way to Cubist-inflected cityscapes of strip malls and industrial sites, placed in the Fairfield Porter circle and read as paintings of modern life with a quiet loneliness. She gave, he writes, "intimacy to vastness".
  Read/Watch: [Max Norman, "The Many Views of Yvonne Jacquette", New York Review of Books](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2026/11/19/the-many-views-of-yvonne-jacquette/) (paywall)

## 📚 Books & Writing
- **Patrick deWitt — *Dodge City*** — Out now from 4th Estate: deWitt's sixth novel, in which Lee Clarke loses his college deferment in 1967, takes a drive-away job delivering a racing-green E-type Jaguar across America and heads for the Canadian border, saying goodbye to each branch of his family on the way. John Quin calls it an "exemplary Vietnam War-era road novel" that "restores your faith in North American decency", with one magnificent insult. A deWitt fan's obvious pick.
  Read/Watch: [John Quin's Irish Times review](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/books/review/2026/10/24/dodge-city-by-patrick-dewitt-a-wise-novel-that-contains-the-best-insult-youll-hear-all-year/) (paywall)
- **Gideon Rachman — *War Is Interested in You*** — Out 22 Oct from Bodley Head: the FT's chief foreign affairs columnist argues the rules-based order is breaking down, strongmen are ascendant, and Taiwan is the likeliest trigger for a US-China war, with the old safeguards (democratic peace, deterrence, institutions) no longer holding. Charlie English finds it anecdotal, engaging and short of fatalism: "While the outlook of War Is Interested in You is undoubtedly gloomy, Rachman's book is not." Snyder and Harari territory.
  Read/Watch: [Charlie English's Guardian review](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2026/oct/26/war-is-interested-in-you-by-gideon-rachman-review-how-close-is-world-war-three)
- **Sam Riviere — *Doppelgänger*** — Out 5 Nov from Weidenfeld & Nicolson: a campus novel set at the Northwick Author Development Academy, where creative-writing students lose their individuality and start "twinning", copying one another, while a hidden system-builder manipulates the literary market and an automated storyteller looms. Riviere's point is that AI did not create literature's originality problem, it exposed one that MFA culture already had. LARB calls it "a courageous experiment that rewards its readers"; dense and theory-heavy, in a good way.
  Read/Watch: [Zachary Smolar, "Endlessly Reproducible Originality", Los Angeles Review of Books](https://lareviewofbooks.org/article/doppelganger-sam-riviere-mfa-writing-programs-generative-ai/)
- **Yoon Ha Lee — *Code and Codex*** — Out 27 Oct from Saga Press (Solaris in the UK): a galactic empire that conquers through language and censorship, an empress's censor-assassin sent to hunt an escaped commander called the Basilisk, ink-and-paper starships and a fragmented, unreliable narration. Linguistics-driven space opera in the Ted Chiang vein; Chris Kluwe says it "requires your undivided attention" and is "worth the time spent uncovering its manifold depths".
  Read/Watch: [Chris Kluwe's Lightspeed review](https://www.lightspeedmagazine.com/?p=36734)
- **Walter Mosley — *Hanging and Burning*** — Out 20 Oct from Little, Brown: the fourth King Oliver novel, in which the New York PI takes a case for his 96-year-old grandmother, a repentant ex-gangster's missing granddaughter and a decades-old secret tied to a vanished jazz singer, leading to a church with peculiar rituals. Kirkus finds it overstuffed but relishes Mosley's "unique blend of family, Black history, and sex"; Booklist says he is "in the zone". Hard-boiled with a historical undertow.
  Read/Watch: [Kirkus review](https://www.kirkusreviews.com/book-reviews/walter-mosley/hanging-and-burning/)

## 🎬 Film & TV

Malkovich won the Volpi Cup at Venice; the AV Club says the leads "make a great match, arguing with each other and fighting against everyone else". UK and US cinemas from 6 Nov (UK previews 4 Nov).

Reviews split on the script. In UK cinemas from today (Studiocanal).

Reviews are divided on pacing, but on Graham they agree: he "oozes such calm, intense menace in Nocturne that the air practically vibrates". First two episodes today, then weekly to 25 Dec.
  Read/Watch: [Nick Schager's Daily Beast review](https://www.thedailybeast.com/obsessed/nocturne-terrifying-emmy-winner-makes-thrilling-new-show-a-must-see)
- **A Bigger Splash (Jack Hazan, 1974)** — Hazan's semi-staged portrait of David Hockney's break-up with Peter Schlesinger and the painting of *Portrait of an Artist (Pool with Two Figures)*, a landmark of queer British cinema that lands differently after Hockney's death in June. The BFI has republished Philip French's 1975 review, which admires how the film's stylised quality mirrors the paintings: "Hazan has decided with great boldness to centre the whole film upon Hockney's art." On MUBI UK from 6 Nov; on the Criterion Channel in the US.
