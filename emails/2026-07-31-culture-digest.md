# Culture Digest — 31 July 2026

A week of returns and farewells: Vini Reilly's first Durutti Column record in fifteen years, a posthumous outsider-folk reverie, Betye Saar's death at 99, Tarkovsky's last film in 4K, and Colson Whitehead closing out the Harlem trilogy.

## 🎵 Music
- **The Durutti Column — *Renascent*** — The first Durutti Column album in over 15 years, made by a man who relearned the guitar after three strokes left him without a sense of touch. Harp-like, spacious, unmistakably Reilly — and a reminder that Frank Ocean, Harry Styles and Blood Orange have all been quietly borrowing from him.
  Read/Watch: [musicOMH review (4.5/5)](https://www.musicomh.com/reviews/albums/the-durutti-column-renascent)
- **Shearwater — *The New World*** — Out today. Four years, three continents, field recordings from Melbourne baggage carousels to Tasmanian birdsong, and Laurie Anderson closing the record with a spoken-word walk on a beach. Talk Talk and The Blue Nile are the obvious touchstones; it's probably their best.
  Read/Watch: [musicOMH review (4.5/5)](https://www.musicomh.com/reviews/albums/shearwater-the-new-world)
- **Nick Granata — *My Heart is Running Backwards! A Martial Artist! With a Closed Fist! Woof!*** — The Shovel Dance Collective member's debut solo LP, played entirely by himself on viola, pump organ, train whistle and a trumpet with no mouthpiece. Folk storytelling turned on the humdrum minutiae of modern life, and constantly on the edge of what's comfortable.
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus — "The Goo Place"](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/album-of-the-week/my-heart-is-running-backwards-a-martial-artist-with-a-closed-fist-woof-nick-granata-review/)
- **Ed Askew — *The Final Painting*** — The outsider-folk poet died in early 2025 at 84; this is what he and Jerry David DeCicca assembled from apartment demos, with Bill Callahan, Sharon Van Etten and William Tyler along for it. Half-sung, half-spoken, jazz brushes and saxophone — an album to sit with whole.
  Read/Watch: [Americana UK review (8/10)](https://americana-uk.com/ed-askew-the-last-painting)
- **Tricky — *Different When It's Silent*** — Six years of silence after the death of his daughter, broken by his sharpest record in decades. Mitch Sanders' falsetto against Tricky's rasp, strings and a wordless soprano; difficult, but it resolves into light.
  Read/Watch: [musicOMH review (4/5)](https://www.musicomh.com/reviews/albums/tricky-different-when-its-silent)

## 🎨 Art
- **Betye Saar (1926–2026)** — Saar died on 26 July, four days short of her 100th birthday, after six decades turning the debris of American racism into mystical, furious assemblages. This obituary is unusually good on the *making* — the Watts Towers as childhood fairy castles, the Cornell show that turned her, the robe of knotted hair that gave her "an electrical shock".
  Read/Watch: [The Art Newspaper obituary](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/07/29/betye-saar-obituary-legendary-los-angeles-assemblage-artist)
- **Edward Hicks's *Peaceable Kingdom* paintings** — Philip Hoare on the Quaker preacher who painted the same lion-and-lamb scene 62 times, never for sale, right up to the day he died. Short, strange and quietly devastating.
  Read/Watch: [Apollo — "The Quaker who painted what he preached"](https://apollo-magazine.com/edward-hicks-peaceable-kingdom-quaker/)
- **Anni Albers and the thinking behind the thread** — Rachel Cohen makes the case for Albers as a conceptual artist rather than a decorative one, and does it through the knot: Dürer's loop drawings, Inca *khipu* as knotted writing, a topologist at Black Mountain College.
  Read/Watch: [Apollo — "The knotty thinking of Anni Albers"](https://apollo-magazine.com/annie-albers-knots-weaving-textiles-belvedere-review/)

## 📚 Books & Writing
- **Colson Whitehead — *Cool Machine*** — The close of the Harlem trilogy drops Ray Carney and Pepper into 1980s New York for one last heist. Bone-chilling violence, deadpan comedy and gentrification-era social criticism in the same breath — the literary end of the crime shelf.
  Read/Watch: [Irish Times review](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/books/review/2026/07/25/cool-machine-by-colson-whitehead-violence-humour-and-sharp-social-criticism/) (paywall)
- **Bill Buford revisits *Among the Thugs*** — Buford on the six years he spent embedded with English football firms, and on what literary narrative journalism is actually for. Doubles as a history of *Granta*'s dirty-realism issue — Carver, Ford, Amis — with Kapuściński and a flying beer can thrown in.
  Read/Watch: [The Paris Review — "The Theater of Sport"](https://www.theparisreview.org/blog/2026/07/29/the-theater-of-sport-bill-buford-revisits-among-the-thugs/)
- **John Lanchester — *Look What You Made Me Do*** — Starts as a precisely observed novel about a marriage and its private language, then turns *Black Mirror* when a hit streaming show begins quoting the couple's most intimate in-jokes back at them. Laura Marsh's review is a proper essay on Lanchester's whole arc from *Capital* to *The Wall*.
  Read/Watch: [New York Review of Books — "Home Invasions"](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2026/08/20/home-invasions-look-what-you-made-me-do-john-lanchester/) (paywall)
- **Nathaniel Rich — *Cloudthief*** — A heist novel in which a broke climate journalist falls for an off-grid saboteur and helps her rob an Oklahoma data centre. Rich makes the invisible infrastructure of "the cloud" physical, and the femme-fatale scaffolding is knowingly noir.
  Read/Watch: [NPR review](https://www.npr.org/2026/07/16/nx-s1-5895124/cloudthief-review-nathaniel-rich-data-centers)

## 🎬 Film & TV
- **The Sacrifice** (Tarkovsky, 4K restoration — Curzon Home Cinema / Klassiki) — Tarkovsky's last completed film, out this week in a new 4K: Bergman's island, Sven Nykvist's drained colour, a man bargaining with God as nuclear annihilation approaches. It has aged into something stranger than it seemed in 1986.
  Read/Watch: [Irish Times review](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/film/review/2026/07/31/the-sacrifice-review-no-mistaking-the-quiet-despair-in-reissue-of-tarkovskys-last-film/)
- **Blue Heron** (Sophy Romvari — The Criterion Channel) — Romvari's semi-autobiographical debut restages a 1990s Hungarian-Canadian childhood around a brother the film never quite lets us see. Akerman-inflected, and precise about how memory overwrites itself. On Criterion since 21 July.
  Read/Watch: [Sight & Sound review](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/blue-heron-grief-not-problem-be-solved-sophy-romvaris-bittersweet-debut)
- **Miroirs No. 3** (Christian Petzold — MUBI) — Petzold's fourth film with Paula Beer: 86 minutes, a piano student walks away from a fatal crash into a stranger's house and quietly takes the place of a dead daughter. *Vertigo* and *Persona* filtered through Brandenburg light.
  Read/Watch: [RogerEbert.com review](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/miroirs-no-3-christian-petzold-film-review-2026)
- **The Death of Robin Hood** (Michael Sarnoski — digital/VOD) — Sarnoski follows *Pig* with the *Unforgiven* of Robin Hood films: Hugh Jackman as an aged, blood-soaked outlaw healing in a Northern Irish priory under Jodie Comer's prioress, the aspect ratio narrowing as the world closes in. Languid, spiritual, pointedly anti-franchise.
  Read/Watch: [RogerEbert.com review](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/the-death-of-robin-hood-hugh-jackman-movie-review-2026)
