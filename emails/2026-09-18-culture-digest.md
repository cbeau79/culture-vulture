# Culture Digest — 18 September 2026

A quiet, handmade week: a Swedish guitarist and a 79-year-old YMO founder both make records that barely raise their voices, Nigel Godrich builds a band out of tape loops, Anish Kapoor gets taken apart, and Emily St. John Mandel splits America in two.

## 🎵 Music

- **Finn Loxbo — *Lummernikt*** — Out today on Fönstret: eight acoustic pieces played live with no overdubs by a Swedish guitarist who spent a decade turning free improvisation into real compositional rigour, with a Gastr del Sol lineage running underneath. The Quietus made it Album of the Week and called one track the most breathtaking thing the reviewer had heard all year.
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus — "Not the How But the What"](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/album-of-the-week/tripping-the-light-fantastic-lummernikt-by-finn-loxbo-review/)
- **Haruomi Hosono — *Yours Sincerely*** — Released 11 Sep on Ghostly International, the Happy End and YMO founder's first original album in nearly a decade: extremely quiet, mostly beatless, drifting between doo-wop, gamelan and ambient miniatures. Owen Hatherley reads it as a deliberately amniotic, consoling record made by the last surviving member of YMO.
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus — "The Beautiful World"](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/album-of-the-week/haruomi-hosono-yours-sincerely-review/)
- **Dragonflies — *Dragonflies*** — Out today on Dark Horse: Dhani Harrison and Nigel Godrich's debut as a duo, built entirely on 24-track analogue tape from loops they played and then sampled off themselves — no computers, no synths. Godrich frames the method as trip-hop construction turned inward; piano loops, David Campbell strings and Joey Waronker on drums do the rest.
  Read/Watch: [SPIN — Harrison and Godrich on becoming Dragonflies](https://www.spinmagazine.com/2026/09/dhani-harrison-nigel-godrich-dragonflies-new-band-feature/)
- **Jake Xerxes Fussell — *The Old Beloved Path*** — Out today on Fat Possum, his sixth album and a live-in-a-room document produced by Ken Whiteley, splicing "Rock Island Line" with a nursery rhyme and turning the sea shanty "Boney" into a slow electric lament. A musicologist's ear applied to pre-war folk and blues that never once sounds like reenactment.
  Read/Watch: [KLOF Mag review](https://klofmag.com/2026/09/jake-xerxes-fussell-the-old-beloved-path/)
- **Kiefer — *Memory Bomb*** — Out 25 Sep on Blue Note: the LA pianist-producer threads jazz piano through hip-hop drums and soul vocals from Rae Khalil, Relyae and Amber Navran, with the standout "Nowhere Fast" pulling an explicit Frank Ocean comparison. The instrumental interludes do as much work as the songs.
  Read/Watch: [Silent Radio review](https://www.silentradio.co.uk/09/14/album-review-kiefer-memory-bomb/)

## 🎨 Art

- **Sandra George — *Start from the Level*** — George was a Black British photojournalist who spent the 1980s and 90s documenting Edinburgh's housing schemes from the inside, and got no mainstream recognition before her death in 2013. The piece carries a good run of the pictures: kids on a timber-pile playground in Leith, an International Women's Day rally, her own unflinching self-portraits as a single mother.
  Read/Watch: [Hyperallergic — "Sandra George's Portraits of Working-Class Dignity"](https://hyperallergic.com/sandra-georges-portraits-of-working-class-dignity/)
- **Anish Kapoor at the Hayward** — A properly sharp dissenting review that asks what's left of Kapoor once you subtract scale and expensive optical tricks, and decides the best thing in the building is a video work by Kulpreet Singh squeezed into a leftover room. Enjoyable purely as argument, and the installation shots of the Vantablack voids do the looking for you.
  Read/Watch: [Hyperallergic — "Anish Kapoor Is a One-Trick Illusionist"](https://hyperallergic.com/anish-kapoor-is-a-one-trick-illusionist/)
- **Georgia Gardner Gray — *Stones*** — Gray paints Stonehenge as a stage rather than a monument, so the stones recede and the crowds take over: figures in tangerine and lemon ponchos watching the sunset through chain-link, skeletons with auras, a harlequin kissing a ghost. The critic has never been to Stonehenge either, which gives the piece a nicely from-a-distance angle.
  Read/Watch: [Frieze — "Georgia Gardner Gray Taps Into the Theatre of Stonehenge"](https://www.frieze.com/article/georgia-gardner-gray-stones-2026-review)

## 📚 Books & Writing

- **Emily St. John Mandel — *Exit Party*** — Out 15 Sep from Knopf: a doppelgänger novel set in a 2031 United States that has split in two after a coup — one half collapsed into micro-nations, the other into an authoritarian surveillance state — with characters pulled forward from *The Glass Hotel*. The A-Side/B-Side structure is doing real formal work on the idea of the counterlife.
  Read/Watch: [Lit Hub — Mandel on writing a novel of dystopian counterlives](https://lithub.com/emily-st-john-mandel-on-writing-a-novel-of-dystopian-counterlives/)
- **Emma Cline — *Switzy*** — A 70-year-old with a dementia diagnosis flies from New York to Zurich and an assisted-dying facility, the prose fracturing as he goes. Booker-longlisted and a genuine swerve from Cline's earlier work — worth reading before the shortlist lands on 22 Sep.
  Read/Watch: [Helen Cullen's review, The Irish Times](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/books/review/2026/09/12/switzy-by-emma-cline-a-remarkable-act-of-imagination-and-empathy/) (paywall)
- **Abby Geni — *Children of the Wolf*** — Ballantine, 15 Sep: two siblings raised in rural isolation by a Viking-obsessed father who turns out to be a serial killer, then twenty quiet years of beekeeping until men start disappearing again. Literary crime with a mythic spine, working the hard question of whether conscience is inborn or installed.
  Read/Watch: [Chicago Review of Books — "The Children Are Not Okay"](https://chireviewofbooks.com/2026/09/15/in-children-of-the-wolf-the-children-are-not-okay/)
- **Jon Ronson — *The Castle: Adventures in a World of Unraveling Men*** — Ronson's son was lured to a Connecticut castle by a stranger running a fake reality show, and the investigation turned into a book about deindustrialisation, dead institutions and men with no structure left to hold onto. Diagnostic rather than prescriptive — the kind of reporting that sits next to Harari and Snyder.
  Read/Watch: [Ronson and Geoff Bennett on modern masculinity, PBS NewsHour (video + transcript)](https://artscanvas.org/arts-culture/jon-ronson-and-geoff-bennett-discuss-modern-masculinity-on-settle-in)

## 🎬 Film & TV

- **Blue Heron** (Sophy Romvari — Criterion Channel in the US now; added to MUBI UK today) — Romvari's debut rebuilds a Hungarian-Canadian family's 1990s Vancouver Island summer around an eldest brother whose behaviour is coming apart, then breaks the frame entirely so the adult filmmaker can walk back into her own memory. The best-reviewed film of the year and the most formally adventurous piece of grief-writing you'll see.
  Read/Watch: [Matthew Eng, Reverse Shot — "You Were Never Really Here"](https://reverseshot.org/reviews/entry/3435/blue_heron)
- **The History of Concrete** (John Wilson — US cinemas, limited release from today) — Wilson's first feature opens as a straight-faced Ken Burns documentary about a building material and slowly curdles into a meta-chronicle of how nearly impossible such a film is to fund, which turns out to be the real subject. Sundance reviewers called it both the funniest thing there and a serious meditation on death and creative doubt.
  Read/Watch: [TheWrap — John Wilson on *The History of Concrete*](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/history-of-concrete-john-wilson-interview/)
- **The Scout** (Paula González-Nasser — opens New York 25 Sep, expanding after) — A location scout spends a day slipping in and out of strangers' apartments, absorbing their loneliness until it's indistinguishable from her own. González-Nasser did the job herself for seven years and cites Akerman's *Les rendez-vous d'Anna* as the model for how little needs to happen.
  Read/Watch: [In Review Online — "Between the Production and Real Life"](https://inreviewonline.com/2025/10/23/paula-gonzalez-nasser-on-the-scout/)
- **Trespasses** (Anthony Byrne, from Louise Kennedy's novel — all four episodes stream from Sun 20 Sep via PBS Passport and PBS Masterpiece on Prime Video) — Lola Petticrew plays Cushla, a Catholic schoolteacher in 1975 Northern Ireland whose affair with a married Protestant barrister runs alongside her entanglement with a family destroyed by sectarian violence. Four IFTAs including Lead Actress and Best Script, with Gillian Anderson as the alcoholic mother.
  Read/Watch: [The Irish Times — "Far more fun than a Troubles drama should be"](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/tv-radio/2025/11/12/trespasses-review-this-is-far-more-fun-than-a-troubles-drama-should-be/)
