# Culture Digest — 25 September 2026

A week of bruised quiet and big swings: Space Afrika add strings to tower-block dub, Julia Jacklin sings about wanting to be left alone, China Miéville returns with a 1,200-page Cold War ghost story, and Zoe Kazan takes on the whole of *East of Eden*.

## 🎵 Music

- **Space Afrika — *Quiet Storm*** — Out today on Dais: the Manchester duo's dusty tower-block dub now has cellos, harp and Kelly Moran's piano, and the extra scale never tips into pomp. "MLN", with Tony Njoku's processed falsetto over a slow full-band march, is the emotional centre.
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus, Album of the Week — Kat Benedict on *Quiet Storm*](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/album-of-the-week/quiet-storm-by-space-afrika-review/)
- **Actress — *Radical Frame*** — Released 18 Sep on Ninja Tune, Darren Cunningham's first proper solo album in over two years: bit-crushed melodies and sad piano stabs, anchored by "Sheets", a 16-minute ambient sprawl with Rainy Miller's voice arriving like an answerphone message.
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus — Antonio Poscic on *Radical Frame*](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/actress-radical-frame-review/)
- **Julia Jacklin — *The Gem*** — Out today on 4AD and recorded above a Melbourne bar: her most honest record, pulled between wanting love and wanting to be left alone, with the closer "I Wish" saying it outright.
  Read/Watch: [Treble — Virginia Croft on *The Gem*](https://www.treblezine.com/julia-jacklin-the-gem-review/)
- **Dani Offline — *Lover's Discourse*** — Out today on Eulipion Arts: an Oakland/LA songwriter-producer's all-analogue, sample-free debut, built on 1970s Quiet Storm radio and Roland Barthes. Jazz-leaning, quietly seductive, each song written as a conversation — very much a deep dig.
  Read/Watch: [OneWrite — "Dani Offline Is Building Something That Lasts" (pre-release interview; no review yet)](https://onewritemusic.substack.com/p/dani-offline-is-building-something)
- **Dungen — *Vidrig Vår*** — Out today: the Swedish psych veterans' barest record, built on Gustav Ejstes's wide piano progressions — a quiet, grown-up album about the end of a long relationship.
  Read/Watch: [Aquarium Drunkard — Justin Gage on *Vidrig Vår*](https://aquariumdrunkard.com/2026/09/24/dungen-vidrig-var/) (paywall)

## 🎨 Art

- **Sunil Gupta, five decades of queer life on camera** — Ben Luke starts from *From Here to Eternity* (1999) — self-portraits after Gupta's HIV diagnosis paired with shuttered gay clubs by day — and argues the work settles art-vs-activism by being both tender and political. A close, lovely reading of individual photographs, timed to his biggest survey yet at Kettle's Yard.
  Read/Watch: [The Art Newspaper — "Sunil Gupta's intimate photographs show how art and activism can work together"](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/09/17/comment-sunil-guptas-intimate-photographs-show-how-art-and-activism-can-work-together)
- **Taryn Simon — *Father Country I Do Love You*** — Simon builds a sprawling investigation around a retired FBI agent who turned up near TWA 800, the Pentagon on 9/11 and the Clinton impeachment, framed by her father's uncheckable spy stories. Aruna D'Souza admires it but pushes back: here injustice is only ever "the system", never people.
  Read/Watch: [Hyperallergic — "No One Is Accountable in Taryn Simon's Detective Story"](https://hyperallergic.com/no-one-is-accountable-in-taryn-simons-detective-story/) (paywall — free sign-in)
- **George Lucas's $1bn Lucas Museum of Narrative Art** — Jori Finkel gives the building five stars and the displays two, and is funniest on the ground-floor gallery of full-size replicas — Altamira bison, the Sistine ceiling, spinning digital Venuses — that feels like being trapped inside the metaverse.
  Read/Watch: [The Art Newspaper — The Big Review: Lucas Museum of Narrative Art](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/09/21/lucas-museum-narrative-art-los-angeles-big-review)

## 📚 Books & Writing

- **China Miéville — *The Rouse*** — His first full solo novel since 2012 (Del Rey, 15 Sep; ~1,260pp): a Cold War ghost story in which a grieving mother and an old soldier get tangled up with a secret British unit that weaponises the dead. Reviewers are reaching for le Carré, Ballard and Bolaño; even the sceptics call it vast.
  Read/Watch: [The Arts Fuse — "The Ghosts of Empire in China Miéville's *The Rouse*"](https://artsfuse.org/333806/book-review-the-ghosts-of-empire-in-china-mievilles-the-rouse/)
- **Luke Kennard — *Black Bag*** — Booker-shortlisted on 22 Sep: a broke actor takes a job sitting silently inside a black leather bag in a lecture hall, restaging a real 1967 psychology experiment. Zadie Smith called it the funniest and smartest novel she'd read in a while — a campus comedy for the end of the humanities.
  Read/Watch: [London Review Bookshop podcast — Luke Kennard with Will Eaves on *Black Bag*](https://www.londonreviewbookshop.co.uk/podcasts-video/podcasts/luke-kennard-and-will-eaves-black-bag)
- **Michael Koryta — *Die Famous*** — Little, Brown, 22 Sep: a Depression-era gangster novel built on Indianapolis's real Brady Gang, told partly through a woman's interrogation on the eve of her execution. The nearest thing to Ellroy-style historical noir this fortnight; Stephen King calls it the best gangster novel he's read.
  Read/Watch: [Bev Vincent's review of *Die Famous*](https://www.bevvincent.com/review/book-review-die-famous-by-michael-koryta/)
- **Kai Bird & Susan Goldmark — *American Scoundrel*** — Fintan O'Toole uses Bird's Roy Cohn biography to argue that Cohn's playbook — lie relentlessly, weaponise the law, sue everybody — has become government policy. A Snyder-style essay on how a democracy's norms rot.
  Read/Watch: [Fintan O'Toole, NYRB — "Devils in America"](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2026/10/08/devils-in-america-roy-cohn-bird-goldmark/) (paywall)

## 🎬 Film & TV

- **East of Eden (Zoe Kazan — all 7 episodes on Netflix, Thu 1 Oct)** — Kazan adapts the whole three-generation Steinbeck saga, seventy years after her grandfather Elia's film covered only its last third, with Florence Pugh as Cathy at its magnetic, poisonous centre. Reviews are held until premiere, so this is a pedigree pick.
  Read/Watch: [TIME — "How Zoe Kazan Made *East of Eden* Her Own"](https://time.com/article/2026/09/16/zoe-kazan-east-of-eden-netflix/)
- **Sense and Sensibility (Georgia Oakley — UK & Irish cinemas from today; US 16 Oct)** — The *Blue Jean* director takes Austen outdoors — wind, mud, dry brush — and finds more melancholy than usual under the wit. Daisy Edgar-Jones and Esmé Creed-Miles make a convincing sister act; critics split on whether it escapes Ang Lee's shadow.
  Read/Watch: [TheWrap — William Bibbiani's review](https://www.thewrap.com/creative-content/movies/sense-and-sensibility-2026-review-daisy-edgar-jones/)
- **Tip Toe (Russell T Davies — Channel 4 streaming in the UK; Starz in the US from Fri 2 Oct)** — Davies's rawest work: a petty row between neighbours on Manchester's Canal Street, with Alan Cumming and David Morrissey at career-best, curdles into a slow-motion horror about online hate spilling into real life. A catch-up for UK viewers, new in the US.
  Read/Watch: [Empire — David Opie's review](https://www.empireonline.com/tv/reviews/tip-toe/)
- **Blue Film (Elliot Tuttle — UK cinemas from today via Peccadillo)** — A fearless two-hander debut: a camboy (Kieron Moore) discovers his masked client is his disgraced former teacher (Reed Birney, unnervingly gentle), and the film refuses you any comfortable moral position. Content note: deals with child abuse.
  Read/Watch: [Slant — Eli Friedberg's review](https://www.slantmagazine.com/film/blue-film-review-elliot-tuttle/)
