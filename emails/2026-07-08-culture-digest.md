## 🎨 Art

- **Njideka Akunyili Crosby — *The Obamas: Springing Forth*** — The first official joint portrait of the Obamas rewards slow looking: buried inside are Michelle's Chicago childhood home, the Oval Office MLK bust, okra and hibiscus, and the 1963 March on Washington.
  Read/Watch: [Hyperallergic — the embedded references decoded](https://hyperallergic.com/the-obamas-unveil-first-ever-official-dual-portrait/)

- **Tuan Andrew Nguyen — *The Light That Shines Through the Universe*** — A 27-foot sandstone Buddha resurrecting one of the Bamiyan figures destroyed in 2001; its hands are cast from brass artillery shells sourced near Bamiyan, floating apart from the body — damage acknowledged, hope intact.
  Read/Watch: [Hyperallergic feature](https://hyperallergic.com/a-buddha-is-reborn-on-the-high-line/)

- **Ana Mendieta — the *Silueta* earth-body works** — Her 200-plus ephemeral "Siluetas," burned and carved into the landscapes of Iowa and Mexico (1973–80), survive only as photographs and film: the female body merging with the earth, tied to her childhood exile from Cuba.
  Read/Watch: [Artsy essay — why the Siluetas land so hard](https://www.artsy.net/article/artsy-editorial-artwork-changed-life-ana-mendietas-silueta-series)

- **Richard Dadd — *The Fairy Feller's Master-Stroke*** — A painter of huge promise who killed his father in 1843 and spent 40-plus years in asylums, producing this hallucinatory, microscopically detailed fairy scene over nine years in Bethlem.
  Read/Watch: [Public Domain Review essay by the RA show's curator](https://publicdomainreview.org/essay/richard-dadds-master-stroke/)

## 📚 Books & Writing

- **Daniel Mason — *Country People*** (Random House, 7 Jul) — His first contemporary-set novel after *North Woods*: a wry campus fable of two academics decamping to rural Vermont, an unfinished dissertation on Russian folklore, and a local hunt for a hollow-earth entrance.
  Read/Watch: [Christian Science Monitor review](https://www.csmonitor.com/Arts-Culture/Books/2026/0707/daniel-mason-country-people-novel)

- **Missouri Williams — *The Vivisectors*** — The follow-up to *The Doloriad*: a gothic, Ballardian campus novel set in a "University" being reclaimed by militant gardeners — a book about life after the collapse of shared meaning.
  Read/Watch: [Defector — a brilliant long review](https://defector.com/the-vivisectors-missouri-williams-review)

- **Michael Connelly — *Ironwood*** — Second in the Catalina Island Detective Stilwell series: an insular procedural with a stakeout gone lethal and cameos from Renée Ballard and Harry Bosch, its storylines kept current rather than nostalgic.
  Read/Watch: [Crime Fiction Lover — 5-star review](https://crimefictionlover.com/2026/05/ironwood-by-michael-connelly/)

- **C. Thi Nguyen — *The Score: How to Stop Playing Somebody Else's Game*** — The philosopher of "value capture" turns on metrics: scoring systems give clarity at the cost of autonomy, quietly substituting the measurable for the meaningful. Squarely in the Meadows/Roth systems-thinking lane.
  Read/Watch: [TLS review by N. J. Enfield](https://www.the-tls.com/politics-society/social-cultural-studies/the-score-c-thi-nguyen-book-review-n-j-enfield) (paywall)

- **Maggie O'Farrell — *Land*** — Her tenth novel: a non-linear Irish saga radiating out from a single windswept peninsula through the Great Hunger, Norse incursions and emigration — structured "like terrain rather than narrative."
  Read/Watch: [Oxford Review of Books review](https://www.the-orb.org/post/in-review-land)

## 🎬 Film & TV

- **The Odyssey** (dir. Christopher Nolan, in cinemas 17 Jul) — Nolan's Homer, with Matt Damon as Odysseus: the first feature shot entirely on IMAX, and by his account the hardest film he's made — a literary-epic swing rather than franchise spectacle.
  Read/Watch: [Nolan interview — making "the most extreme version"](https://www.hollywoodreporter.com/movies/movie-news/christopher-nolan-the-odyssey-movie-matt-damon-1236598406/)

- **Miroirs No. 3** (dir. Christian Petzold, MUBI, 17 Jul) — Petzold reunites with Paula Beer for a hushed four-hander: a piano student survives a crash and is quietly absorbed into a grieving family. *Vertigo* and *Persona* in a minor key.
  Read/Watch: [Roger Ebert review](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/miroirs-no-3-christian-petzold-film-review-2026)

- **April** (dir. Dea Kulumbegashvili, MUBI, 24 Jul) — The Georgian director's Venice Special Jury Prize-winner: a formalist, unblinking portrait of an obstetrician who secretly performs abortions, shot in long takes and laced with an eerie humanoid figure.
  Read/Watch: [MUBI Notebook interview](https://mubi.com/en/notebook/posts/you-can-t-look-away-dea-kulumbegashvili-on-april)

- **Time and Water** (dir. Sara Dosa, UK cinemas now) — The *Fire of Love* director's elegy for Iceland's vanishing glaciers, framed through writer Andri Snær Magnason: a handcrafted, un-preachy climate documentary with epic scope.
  Read/Watch: [Sight & Sound review](https://www.bfi.org.uk/sight-and-sound/reviews/time-water-icelands-vanishing-glaciers-haunt-this-moving-climate-essay)

- **The Quince Tree Sun** (Víctor Erice, 1992 — a rediscovery) — Sight & Sound's new "Lost and Found": Erice's portrait of painter Antonio López García trying, and failing, to capture the light on a quince tree before it rots. A quietly perfect meditation on representation.
  Read/Watch: [Senses of Cinema essay](https://www.sensesofcinema.com/2025/cteq/passing-light-victor-erices-the-quince-tree-sun/)
