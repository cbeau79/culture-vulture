# Culture Digest — 11 September 2026

A drone-heavy week: Adam Wiltzie finishes a record with the late Jóhann Jóhannsson, Arab Strap go big, Marlon James returns to Jamaica, the Bayeux Tapestry lands in London, and Slow Horses saddles up for its darkest run.

## 🎵 Music

- **Adam Wiltzie & Jóhann Jóhannsson — *Friendship Survival Guide*** — Out 18 Sep on Deutsche Grammophon: the Stars of the Lid co-founder completes a decade-old collaboration with the late Jóhannsson, built from tape loops left over from their *Arrival* sessions, with guitars recorded under Steve Albini. Orchestral drone, choir and organ — squarely in your Frahm/Hopkins lane.
  Read/Watch: [A Closer Listen review](https://acloserlisten.com/2026/09/11/adam-wiltzie-johann-johannsson-friendship-survival-guide/)
- **Agnes Obel — *The Meaning of Flowers*** — First album in six years from the Danish piano-and-voice composer (18 Sep, DG), written and produced alone in her Berlin studio after the birth of her daughter, drawing on Enheduanna, Hannah Arendt and 70s film scores. Introspective chamber-pop between the modern-classical and singer-songwriter strands.
  Read/Watch: [The Line of Best Fit interview — "Agnes Obel has discovered a different way of seeing"](https://www.thelineofbestfit.com/features/interviews/agnes-obel-has-discovered-a-different-way-of-seeing)
- **Arab Strap — *Half-Told Tales*** — Aidan Moffat and Malcolm Middleton's tenth album (out 4 Sep) is the biggest-sounding of their career, but the Quietus singles out the small, vulnerable songs — a walk through a childhood home now up for sale; raising a son in the manosphere era — as the ones that land. Scottish, literate, melancholic.
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus review](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/arab-strap-half-told-tales-review/)
- **This Is Lorelei — *The Singer in My Band*** — Nate Amos (Water From Your Eyes) turns fully toward folk, country and Gordon Lightfoot-style storytelling, with explicit Lemonheads and Gene Clark nods and his bluegrass-musician father on banjo. Out today on Matador; Stereogum's Album of the Week and Pitchfork's top score this week.
  Read/Watch: [Paste review](https://www.pastemagazine.com/music/this-is-lorelei/this-is-lorelei-the-singer-in-my-band-review)
- **corto.alto — *SOME SMALL FORTUNE*** — Glasgow trombonist-producer Liam Shortall's Ninja Tune debut (4 Sep), with Fergus McCreadie in the band and soulful guest vocals from anaiis, Oscar Jerome and Jacob Alon. Nu-jazz with a strong alt-R&B pull — The Skinny gave it five stars and hears Robert Glasper in the standout "APRIL".
  Read/Watch: [The Skinny review](https://www.theskinny.co.uk/music/reviews/albums/corto-alto-some-small-fortune)

## 🎨 Art

- **Tschabalala Self — *Lady in Blue* (Fourth Plinth)** — Jonathan Jones goes from bored-by-the-speeches to smitten in a paragraph: a painted-bronze striding woman in lapis blue that pulls in Boccioni, Bronzino's ultramarine and Ife bronzes, plus a sharp bit on what a public statue can and can't do in a tense political moment. Photos are everywhere, so it reads well from the sofa.
  Read/Watch: [Jonathan Jones's review, The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/artanddesign/2026/sep/10/tschabalala-self-lady-in-blue-review-fourth-plinth-trafalgar-square-london)
- **The Bayeux Tapestry at the British Museum** — Less about the diplomatic saga than a scene-by-scene look at the embroidery itself: wind-blown hair on galloping messengers, looters stripping armour in the battle chaos, chainmail rendered as circles, with the reviewer's own close-ups. An armchair walk-through of the object.
  Read/Watch: [Artnet review — "Worth the Wait!"](https://news.artnet.com/art-world/bayeux-tapestry-british-museum-review-2807802)
- **Betty Parsons, the dealer as painter** — The legendary gallerist of Pollock, Rothko and Newman never showed her own work in her gallery; a retrospective co-curated by Amy Sillman argues she was a serious colourist — airy 1960s acrylics and driftwood totems. A compact, image-rich introduction to a body of work almost nobody has seen.
  Read/Watch: [Frieze review](https://www.frieze.com/article/betty-parsons-an-expanded-world-2026-review)

## 📚 Books & Writing

- **Marlon James — *The Disappearers*** — His first Jamaica-set novel since *A Brief History of Seven Killings*: a furious, formally restless epic (diaries, letters, interviews) about a gay theatre troupe in 1980s Kingston, which turns into a missing-persons mystery in its middle third. Booker-longlisted and the literary event of the fortnight — fair warning, Ron Charles calls it both great and an ordeal.
  Read/Watch: [Ron Charles — "Marlon James and the Horrors of Vanishing"](https://roncharles.substack.com/p/marlon-james-and-the-horrors-of-vanishing)
- **Kate Atkinson — *Our Noble Selves*** — A gentle noirish mystery around the 1951 Festival of Britain: ex-war correspondent Harry Flynn wakes covered in blood after a date with a Parisian who has vanished, amid fascists, spies and a corrupt festival-cake competition. Postwar hard-boiled with wit, sitting neatly between your literary and crime shelves. Out 10 Sep.
  Read/Watch: [Lucy Atkins's review, The Guardian](https://www.theguardian.com/books/2026/sep/07/our-noble-selves-by-kate-atkinson-review-quirky-postwar-nostalgia-from-the-life-after-life-author)
- **Ben Macintyre — *Redwood*** — KGB officer Vladimir Kuzichkin turns double agent for MI6 in post-revolution Tehran, 1981, foiling a Soviet plot to seize Iran — with a very human, un-Bond motive for defecting. Clare Mulley's review is the substantive UK take, and honest about whether the ending was triumph or disaster. Out 10 Sep.
  Read/Watch: [Clare Mulley's review, The Spectator](https://www.spectator.com.au/2026/09/double-jeopardy-the-story-of-historys-most-audacious-kgb-defector/)
- **Premee Mohamed — *Wickhills*** — A le Carré-style spy story inside a dark-fantasy port city: jaded agent Vycol Ferec must protect a defecting scientist while the state's founding myths curdle around him. Noir-fantasy with bottomless worldbuilding in a compact 320 pages — one for the Abercrombie shelf. Out 8 Sep, Tor.
  Read/Watch: [Nerds of a Feather review](http://www.nerds-feather.com/2026/08/book-review-wickhills-by-premee-mohamed.html)

## 🎬 Film & TV

- **Better Go Mad in the Wild** (Miro Remo — UK cinemas from today) — A hybrid doc about two bearded, bickering twin hermits on a Šumava farm, narrated by one of their cows, that becomes a moving meditation on brotherhood and mortality. Karlovy Vary winner and the most singular thing opening this week.
  Read/Watch: [TheWrap review](https://www.thewrap.com/better-go-mad-in-the-wild-review-czech-documentary-is-the-years-best-twin-movie/)
- **If I Go Will They Miss Me** (Walter Thompson-Hernández — US cinemas from today) — A magical-realist coming-of-age debut set in Watts, threading Greek myth and the Flying Africans folklore through a father-son story, with Danielle Brooks anchoring it. Four stars from RogerEbert.com, which places it in the Charles Burnett / Barry Jenkins lineage.
  Read/Watch: [RogerEbert.com review](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/if-i-go-will-they-miss-me-danielle-brooks-film-review-2026)
- **Oasis: Don't Look Back in Anger** (Lovelace & Southern — cinemas/IMAX from today) — Not a tell-all but a film about what music does for people, shot across the 2025 reunion tour by the *Shut Up and Play the Hits* team. Matt Zoller Seitz calls it one of the best music documentaries ever made.
  Read/Watch: [Matt Zoller Seitz's review](https://www.rogerebert.com/reviews/oasis-dont-look-back-in-anger-concert-film-review)
- **Slow Horses, season 6** (Apple TV — from Wed 16 Sep, weekly) — New head writer Gaby Chiappe adapts *Joe Country* and *Slough House*, with a hit list working through current and former Slough House agents. Early reviews call it the show's best and darkest run.
  Read/Watch: [Tom's Guide full-season review](https://www.tomsguide.com/entertainment/apple-tv/slow-horses-season-6-review-one-of-the-10-best-shows-of-2026-so-far)
