# Culture Digest — 23 August 2026

Late-summer riches: Julia Holter's companion album, Lambchop gone hymnal with Bon Iver's help, a farewell to painter Mary Heilmann, and Claire Denis back with a taut nocturnal standoff.

## 🎵 Music

- **Julia Holter — *Materia*** — Out Friday on Domino: a seven-track companion to 2024's *Something in the Room She Moves*, mixing four new songs written with her touring band and radical reworkings of that album's title track. Slighter than a full statement, but a concentrated dose of her exhilarating art-pop invention.
  Read/Watch: [Paste review — "Materia reveals new mysteries in Julia Holter's voice"](https://www.pastemagazine.com/music/julia-holter/julia-holter-materia-review)
- **Lambchop — *Punching the Clown*** — Kurt Wagner's seventeenth album, cut in three days at Justin Vernon's studio with Vernon on banjo and a six-part choir working the ancient Scottish "lined-out singing" tradition into Wagner's murmured americana. Weird and sedate on first pass; spine-tingling hymnal beauty once it settles.
  Read/Watch: [Mojo review](https://www.mojo4music.com/articles/new-music/lambchop-punching-the-clown-review/)
- **James Ellis Ford — *Lost In Another World*** — The Simian Mobile Disco man and producer behind half of British art rock's best records, with a solo album recorded at London's St Bartholomew's Hospital that lays bare a journey of sickness and recovery. A Quietus Album of the Week.
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus — "Trust in the Process"](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/album-of-the-week/trusting-in-the-process-lost-in-another-world-by-james-ellis-ford/)
- **L'Rain — *fata morgana*** — Taja Cheek's drifting, collaged art-soul earned the strongest reviews of any record in the alternative R&B lane this month — squarely in your Frank Ocean/Sampha territory, and Stereogum's Album of the Week.
  Read/Watch: [Stereogum Album of the Week](https://stereogum.com/2507540/album-of-the-week-lrain-fata-morgana/reviews/album-of-the-week)
- **Šimanský Vaľko Podracký — *K-drone*** — Three explorers from the Czech-Slovak experimental scene burrow deep into the woodland in search of heady acoustic drones and, just maybe, a new experience of time. The deepest of this week's deep cuts, and the Quietus's current Album of the Week.
  Read/Watch: [The Quietus — "Fuzzy Logic"](https://thequietus.com/quietus-reviews/album-of-the-week/simansky-valko-podracky-k-drone-review/)

## 🎨 Art

- **Mary Heilmann (1940–2026)** — The Californian who dragged surf culture, ceramics and day-glo colour into hard-edged abstraction died on 14 August at 86, after a Whitney farewell show that closed only in April. This obituary is sharp on why her "irreverent" paintings took decades to get their due — and why younger painters adore her.
  Read/Watch: [The Art Newspaper obituary](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/08/17/mary-heilmann-fearless-colourist-singular-painter-obituary)
- **Simone Forti on Yoko Ono, dance and aging** — A never-before-published interview with the late postmodern dance pioneer, conducted by art historian Julia Bryan-Wilson. "I remember sitting with Yoko on a ledge on the PS1 roof and talking together comfortably. It was restful."
  Read/Watch: [Hyperallergic interview](https://hyperallergic.com/simone-forti-talks-yoko-ono-dance-and-aging-in-never-before-published-interview/) (paywall)

## 📚 Books & Writing

- **Chang-rae Lee — *A Tender Age*** — The *Native Speaker* author's luminous bildungsroman about the first son of Korean immigrants in 1970s Westchester, and the week's most rapturously reviewed novel (ten raves and counting). Less about one pivotal childhood moment than the accretion of regret and the sweep of aging itself.
  Read/Watch: [Alta review — "Immigrant Memory"](https://www.altaonline.com/books/fiction/a73357880/books-fiction-chang-rae-lee-a-tender-age-korean-american-suburbia/)
- **Téa Obreht — *Sunrise*** — Obreht exults in emulating and then subverting tales of the Old West in a layered, intertextual survival story that critics are calling breathtaking and shocking. This interview digs into how she built it.
  Read/Watch: [Lit Hub interview with Téa Obreht](https://lithub.com/tea-obreht-on-writing-a-layered-intertextual-tale-of-the-american-west/)
- **Ashley Kahn — *Days of Miracle and Wonder*** — A decades-in-the-research account of Paul Simon's *Graceland*: the trials, the boycott politics, and the triumphs. Peerless research with a real musical sensibility — one for replaying your favourite tracks alongside.
  Read/Watch: [Open Letters Review](https://openlettersreview.com/posts/days-of-miracle-and-wondery-by-ashely-kahn)
- **"Freud in the Age of Therapy-Speak"** — A Hedgehog Review essay on what the original talking cure still has to say in an era when therapy vocabulary has colonised everyday speech. A good, chewy read for the philosophy-and-meaning shelf.
  Read/Watch: [The Hedgehog Review essay](https://hedgehogreview.com/issues/the-fading-promise-of-higher-education/articles/freud-in-the-age-of-therapy-speak)

- **It Ends** (Alexander Ullom — in cinemas via NEON) — A debut that RogerEbert.com says "confidently declares a new voice in the horror sphere": an endless-road nightmare built on Gen-Z angst and liminal dread. The week's horror conversation piece.

- **The Fence** (Claire Denis) — Denis adapts Koltès's play *Black Battles with Dogs* into a real-time, single-night standoff at a West African construction site: Matt Dillon's evasive foreman against Isaach de Bankolé's quiet, unbuyable demand for his brother's body. - **Lanterns** (HBO Max) — DC's Green Lantern series plays it as slow-burn character drama: Kyle Chandler and Aaron Pierre as two generations of cop-adjacent spacemen in small-town America. The review makes the case that it anchors superhero storytelling to the human condition.
