## 🎨 Art
- **Is the Arnolfini couple actually Van Eyck and his wife?** — The National Gallery's survey of every surviving Van Eyck portrait opens on 21 Nov, and the catalogue carries a proper attribution row: Berlin's Stephan Kemperdick argues the 1434 Arnolfini Portrait shows Jan van Eyck himself with his wife Margaret, reviving a theory Charles Eastlake floated in the 1840s. The curators call it "speculative", and Till-Holger Borchert's verdict is "Can you tell me anything about Van Eyck that isn't enigmatic?"
  Read/Watch: [J.S. Marcus in The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/11/16/has-the-pair-in-jan-van-eycks-arnolfini-portrait-been-misidentified)
- **Robin Simon on what Van Eyck actually changed** — The contrarian companion piece: Simon argues the usual claims for Van Eyck are overstated ("artists had been celebrated individuals long before Van Eyck"), that the real revolution was naturalistic oil paint and the freedom to paint secular sitters, and that Hockney's camera lucida theory would explain the oddly small bodies in portraits like that of Margaret van Eyck. "A revolution as radical as Van Eyck's was as hard to control as any other."
  Read/Watch: [Robin Simon's essay in The Spectator](https://www.spectator.com.au/?p=10947889) (paywall)
- **AI helps conservators recover a trimmed, faded William Merritt Chase** — At the Parrish Art Museum, conservators overlaid a pre-1960s black-and-white photograph of Chase's *A Comfortable Corner* (c. 1888) on a colour image to flag where a curl of hair, part of a decorative screen and the signature had faded or been cropped, then checked each area by hand. A clear-headed account of what the tool did and did not do: "AI is none of those things", says conservator Siano of claims that it is a connoisseur or a scientist.
  Read/Watch: [Annabel Keenan in The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/11/13/ai-helps-conservators-uncover-lost-details-in-william-merritt-chase-painting)
- **Brice Marden's late turn** — Susan Tallman on the Gagosian survey and the new catalogue raisonné, arguing that the famous calm ("optical balance so solid it felt inevitable") gave way to gestural, physically demanding late work, and that Marden's art "was never a celebration of autonomy; it was an atlas of connection". Includes a useful warning for anyone reading about paintings from a sofa: "'knowing' them through JPEGs isn't knowing them at all."
  Read/Watch: [Susan Tallman in the New York Review of Books](https://www.nybooks.com/articles/2026/12/03/seeing-through-the-lightness-brice-marden/) (paywall)

## 📚 Books & Writing
- **Jonathan Coe — *Lessons in Harmony*** — Out 12 Nov from Viking: Coe's sixteenth novel fictionalises the 1907 to 1908 Paris friendship between Ravel and Vaughan Williams, told by a music-critic narrator through found manuscripts and mock-scholarly footnotes, a comedy of Anglo-French misunderstanding with the First World War coming up behind it. Ian Thomson in The Spectator: "Lessons in Harmony has a serious edge and is often moving to read."
  Read/Watch: [Ian Thomson's review in The Spectator](https://www.spectator.com.au/2026/11/a-cultural-alliance-lessons-in-harmony-by-jonathan-coe-reviewed/) (paywall)
- **Hannah Lillith Assadi — *Paradiso 17*** — Winner of the National Book Award for Fiction on Wednesday night: a Palestinian man displaced in 1948 recalls a life across Damascus, Kuwait, Italy, New York and Arizona from his deathbed, in chapters whose titles double as their closing lines. Eman Quotah calls Sufien an "unforgettable, believable, all-too-human character". Knopf in the US; no UK edition confirmed yet.
  Read/Watch: [Eman Quotah's review in The Markaz Review](https://themarkaz.org/ehna-rajiun-a-review-of-hannah-lilith-assadis-paradiso-17/)
- **Michael Connelly — *The Hollow*** — UK 5 Nov from Orion: the twenty-sixth Bosch novel sends Harry back to MacLaren Children's Center, the care home where he grew up, to reopen the death of a boy after a class-action settlement letter arrives. An origin story in which, as Ray Palen puts it, "the line between justice and revenge starts to blur"; Palen thinks it "may be one of the finest books that Connelly has ever written".
  Read/Watch: [Ray Palen's review at Bookreporter](https://admin.bookreporter.com/reviews/the-hollow-a-bosch-novel)
- **Nick Harkaway — *The Taper Man*** — Viking, UK 5 Nov, US 17 Nov: le Carré's son's second Smiley novel after *Karla's Choice*, set in 1965 and largely in Los Angeles, where a sleeper agent known as the Taper Man is waking up. Andrew Rosenheim in The Spectator finds "the writing throughout is evocative and adroit, though there are occasional signs of haste"; Book Marks has it at "Rave" across seven reviews.

- **Mike Jay — *Wireheads: An Unnatural History of Technology in the Brain*** — Yale, out this month: two centuries of mind-control fantasy and fact sorted into five types (Puppet Master, Orgasmatron, God Helmet, Psychotronic Weapon, Reality Simulator), with a lot of Philip K.
