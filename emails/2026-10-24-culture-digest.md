## 🎨 Art
- **Edward Hopper's "first" oil paintings were copies** — In an excerpt from his new Yale biography, Louis Shadwick shows that the works long treated as Hopper's earliest original oils, including *Old Ice Pond at Nyack*, were copied from reproductions in a hobbyist magazine that printed painting instructions for amateurs; the Nyack title was added later by the painting's first owner. A quiet rewrite of the origin story of America's most mythologised painter.
  Read/Watch: [Louis Shadwick in The Art Newspaper](https://www.theartnewspaper.com/2026/10/20/edward-hopper-early-oils-all-copies-louis-shadwick-excerpt-hopper-biography)
- **David Salle lets AI into the studio** — The painter describes training a model only on his own 1999-2001 *Pastoral* paintings ("I sent the machine to art school"), printing its swirling outputs on canvas and painting over them. His argument: AI predicts the probable, good painting depends on the unpredictable, and the real danger is audiences adopting algorithmic taste. A rare first-person account from a major painter rather than a pundit.
  Read/Watch: [David Salle in Frieze](https://www.frieze.com/article/david-salle-2026-ai-painting-opinion)
- **Sophie Calle — *Something Missing?*** — Lisette May Monroe reads Calle as an "editor of life" who builds elaborate systems to hold difficult feeling at arm's length: lonely-hearts ads from 1895 to 2010 paired with infrared photographs of nocturnal animals, a catalogue of abandoned projects each stamped with the reason it failed, and a film of inland Turks seeing the sea for the first time. The critic's resentment at being moved is the point.
  Read/Watch: [Frieze review of the Turner Contemporary survey](https://www.frieze.com/article/sophie-calle-something-missing-2026-review)

## 📚 Books & Writing
- **Yiyun Li — *Music Against the Night*** — Out this month from 4th Estate: the first of a "Nocturne Trilogy" about John Field, the Dublin-born Romantic composer who invented the nocturne, and the French-Indian pianist he unhappily married in Moscow. Li says she "met John Field in Tolstoy's autobiography"; Kirkus calls it "a virtuoso performance". Historical literary fiction with a Dublin thread.
  Read/Watch: [Niamh Donnelly's Irish Times interview with Yiyun Li](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/books/2026/10/24/yiyun-li-on-her-sons-deaths-by-suicide-you-can-carry-on-being-an-alcoholic-or-you-can-carry-on-writing/)
- **Eoin McNamee — *The Comets: Collected Short Fiction*** — From the author of *Resurrection Man*, the great Northern Irish noir: compact, unsettling stories of poverty, sectarianism and hard labour that slide from realism into the eerie (the dead becoming comets over a seaside town). The reviewer praises the "economical menace" of the dialogue: "If there was a realm of bad choices she would be queen." Short fiction with a noir edge.
  Read/Watch: [NJ McGarrigle's Irish Times review](https://www.irishtimes.com/culture/books/review/2026/10/19/the-comets-by-eoin-mcnamee-enlightenment-in-darkness/)
- **Haruki Murakami — *Abandoning a Cat*** — Out 20 Oct from Harvill, 96 illustrated pages expanding his essay about his father: a childhood memory of abandoning a cat that beat them home, the father's conscription and account of a POW beheading, and a suspicion about Nanjing that Murakami put off researching until after his father died. "Quietly devastating", and a memoir about what cannot be known.
  Read/Watch: [Priscilla Gilman's Boston Globe review](https://www.bostonglobe.com/2026/10/20/arts/haruki-murakami-abandoning-cat/)
- **Chad Harbach — *The Brightness*** — Out 27 Oct: the 15-years-later follow-up to *The Art of Fielding*, with Pella Affenlight now 27 and planning a wedding while a campus prank spirals during the 2015-16 activism year; Edith Wharton stands in as the presiding author the way Melville did in the first book. B.D. McClay: "Harbach is a gifted observer of undergraduates, their pathos and their comedy."
  Read/Watch: [B.D. McClay's New Yorker review](https://www.newyorker.com/magazine/2026/10/26/the-brightness-chad-harbach-book-review) (paywall)
- **Nicholas Mulder — *The Age of Confiscation*** — Out 29 Oct from Allen Lane, 768 pages arguing that expropriation, usually filed under dictators and revolutions, has been central to Western history from the ending of serfdom and slavery to industrialisation and the welfare state: states succeeded not by abolishing confiscatory power but by taming it. Big-idea economic history from the author of *The Economic Weapon*.
  Read/Watch: [Erik Linstrum, "States on the Take", Literary Review](https://literaryreview.co.uk/states-on-the-take) (paywall)

- **Fatherland (Paweł Pawlikowski)** — Summer 1949: Thomas Mann and his daughter Erika drive a black Buick across divided Germany to collect Goethe prizes in Frankfurt and Weimar, with Sandra Hüller as Erika keeping her father "safe and happy, mainly with cigars and blunt editorial pointers". Cannes Best Director; 82 minutes, black and white. In UK and US cinemas via Mubi from 23 Oct, streaming date to follow.

Christina Newland calls it a minor-key piece of film history about love, ambition and being a woman artist. In cinemas now, on Mubi from 6 Nov.
  Read/Watch: [Christina Newland's Little White Lies review](https://www.lwlies.com/reviews/making-marie-antoinette)

The New Statesman calls it "the first project of a generational talent"; the Guardian is cooler. Streaming on Channel 4 from 22 Oct.
